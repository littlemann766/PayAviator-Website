Connected accounts

Connected financial accounts

PayAviator uses Plaid to let users connect supported financial institutions. When a user chooses to connect an account, PayAviator may access account and balance information and transaction information made available through Plaid so the app can display financial activity and support budgeting and money-management features.

PayAviator does not ask users to provide their online-banking password directly to PayAviator. Account connections are initiated through Plaid. Users control whether they connect an account and can disconnect connected accounts.

Connecting an account requires a PayAviator account (sign in with Google or email). Bills you add stay on your phone. Connected bank data (synced transactions and connection details) is stored on PayAviator's servers so it can sync. Plaid access tokens are encrypted there and never sent to your phone, and deleting your PayAviator account removes your bank connections at Plaid and deletes that stored data.

For more information, read the PayAviator Privacy Policy.

No. Connecting a financial account is optional. You can enter bills, income, budgets and debts yourself without a PayAviator account. An account (Google or email sign-in) is only needed to connect a bank, and you can disconnect connected accounts at any time.

Safe to Spend starts from Available now, the available balance of the checking and cash accounts you include. It then subtracts your Payday Cushion, which is a percentage of your most recent paycheck (20% by default) or a dollar amount you set. Savings accounts are protected by default; turn on "Include savings in Safe to Spend" to count them. The result never goes below $0. Without connected accounts, Available now comes from PayAviator's own records of checks and funds received, minus paid bills, manual spending and savings.

No. PayAviator does not sell users' personal financial information. See the Privacy Policy for details.

In PayAviator's Payoff Planner you look at one card at a time and choose how to pay it. Snowball pays the card's minimum payment (if you haven't saved one, PayAviator uses 3% of the balance, at least $25). You can add an extra monthly payment: it goes to your smallest balance first, and when that balance is paid off, its payment rolls over to the next one. Snowball shows the projected payoff date, the number of months and the projected interest. Avalanche shows paying the card's full balance off, including the month's interest. Both show how much of the card's credit limit is used now and after the payment. These are estimates, not financial advice. Try the demo.
